Introducing Econometrics

What the subject is for, and why every economic relationship needs an error term.

By the end of this unit you should be able to
  • say what econometrics is, and which four disciplines it draws on;
  • take an economic theory and carry it through to an econometric model;
  • explain why the disturbance term u is not a mistake but a necessity;
  • tell cross-sectional, time series, pooled and panel data apart, and write each correctly;
  • set out the stages of an applied econometric study in order.

1 · What econometrics is

Read literally, econometrics means "economic measurement". That reading is deceptively simple — the practice of the subject is much wider in scope. A fuller definition:

Definition Econometrics consists of the application of mathematical statistics to economic data to lend empirical support to the models constructed by mathematical economics and to obtain numerical results.1

Unpack that definition and econometrics turns out to sit at the meeting point of four separate things: economic theory, which tells you what relationships to expect; mathematical economics, which writes those relationships as equations; mathematical statistics, which supplies the estimation and testing machinery; and economic statistics, which supplies the data. Take away any one of the four and you cannot do the work.

In practice the subject gets used for four things:

1Describing economic reality. How large is the effect of income on consumption in Barbados, in dollars?
2Testing economic theories. Keynes says the marginal propensity to consume lies between zero and one. Does it?
3Forecasting economic activity. Given next year's projected income, what consumption should we expect?
4Evaluating policies. If the tax rate changes, what happens — and by how much?

Notice that all four demand a number, not a direction. Economic theory will happily tell you that consumption rises with income. It will not tell you whether the figure is 0.6 or 0.9, and the difference between those two matters enormously for policy. Supplying the number is the job of econometrics.

2 · From a theory to a model you can estimate

Empirical analysis moves through four stages, and it is worth seeing them with one example carried the whole way.

Stage 1 · Economic theory

Theory suggests important relationships, often with policy implications, but without the quantitative magnitude of the effect. Keynes postulated that

Keynes' conjecture The marginal propensity to consume (MPC) — the rate of change of consumption for a unit change in income — is greater than zero but less than one.

Keynes gave no functional form, so at this stage the relationship can only be written informally:

Consumption = f(income)

That is all theory provides: the rationale for why these variables should move together, and the sign of the relationship.

Stage 2 · The mathematical model

The role of mathematical economics is to take the conjecture and give it a definite form. The simplest choice is a straight line:

Y = β1 + β2X

where Y is consumption expenditure and X is income. The unknowns β1 and β2 are the parameters: β1 is the intercept and β2 is the slope — which, in this model, is the marginal propensity to consume. Keynes' conjecture has now become a testable statement about a single number: 0 < β2 < 1.

Notation — fix this now In this course β1 is always the intercept and β2 the slope, and in multiple regression the regressors carry matching subscripts: β2X2i + β3X3i. Wooldridge, one of the recommended texts, numbers from zero instead — β0 for the intercept, β1 for the first slope. The mathematics is identical; only the labels differ. When you read Wooldridge, translate as you go, and use this course's numbering in everything you submit.

But there is a problem with the mathematical model, and it is fatal. It asserts a deterministic relationship: given income, consumption follows exactly. Every household in the country with the same income would spend precisely the same amount. Perfect relationships of that kind do not exist among economic variables, and a model that claims one cannot be tested against data that contradicts it on every single observation.

Stage 3 · The econometric model

To allow for inexact relationships, the econometrician modifies the equation:

Yi = β1 + β2Xi + ui

Definition u is the disturbance, or error term: a random (stochastic) variable with well-defined probabilistic properties. It represents all those factors that affect consumption but are not taken into account explicitly.

This equation is an econometric model — more precisely, a linear regression model, which is the major concern of this course.

The move from Stage 2 to Stage 3 is the single most important idea in this unit, so it is worth doing rather than reading. Below is the same consumption function twice. Start with the disturbance set to zero and you have the mathematical model of Stage 2: every household sits exactly on the line. Increase it and you have the econometric model.

Two things are worth noticing as you move the slider. First, the line does not move. It is the average relationship, and adding disturbances scatters observations around it without changing it. That average relationship has a name and a notation you will use constantly from Unit 1B onwards:

E(Y | X) = β1 + β2X

Read it as "the expected value of Y, given X". Second, the disturbance for each household is the vertical distance from its point to the line — not the perpendicular distance. That detail matters when you meet least squares in Unit 1B.

What is actually inside u

The disturbance is not a confession of failure. It is a container, and it is worth knowing what it holds, because several later units are about one of these leaking into your results:

iOmitted variables. Consumption depends on wealth, household size and expectations too. None are in the model.
iiIncorrect specification. The true relationship may not be a straight line. Taken up in Unit 2A Part 2.
iiiErrors in measurement. Reported income is not true income.
ivIntrinsic randomness. Human behaviour is simply not perfectly predictable.

A practical wrinkle: proxies

When specifying an econometric model we often have to use proxies for the variables the theory names, because the exact data cannot be found. The Phillips curve holds that the expected rate of inflation influences current inflation. But how would you measure inflation expectations? What data would you actually use? Every applied study faces a version of this question, and the answer you choose becomes part of what your results mean.

Stage 4 · Data

Having specified the model we want to estimate it, and to carry out statistical inference we must have data. Which raises the question of where data come from, and what kind of real process generates them.

3 · Data

Experimental and observational data

In an experimental study, researchers impose treatments and controls, then observe the characteristic of interest and take measurements. In an observational study, researchers simply observe what is happening, or has happened in the past, and try to draw conclusions from it. Observational studies examine variables as they were observed and recorded.

The consequence for everything that follows Economists work in a complex world where data on variables are "observed" and rarely obtained from a controlled experiment. With observational data, cause and effect are hard — often impossible — to establish. Associations and predictabilities among variables can still be investigated, and that is what this course teaches you to do. Keep the distinction in mind whenever you are tempted to describe a regression coefficient as an effect.

Types of variable, types of dataset

Variables are either qualitative (gender, race — categories) or quantitative (data you can perform mathematical operations on). Qualitative variables can still be used in a regression, but they need special handling, which is the subject of Unit 3A.

Datasets come in four kinds, and the methods you are allowed to use depend on which one you have:

1Cross-sectional. Many units, one point in time. 40 households surveyed this year.
2Time series. One unit, many periods. Barbadian aggregate consumption, 1970 to 2016.
3Pooled cross sections. Independent cross-sections from different periods, stacked together. A fresh sample of households each year.
4Panel (longitudinal). The same units followed over several periods. The same 40 households, every year for ten years.
Why this is not just terminology Econometric methods depend on the nature of the data used, and the use of inappropriate methods may lead to misleading results. Autocorrelation (Unit 2D) is a time series problem; heteroscedasticity (Unit 2C) is most often a cross-sectional one. Identifying your data type is the first step in choosing a method.

Notation

The subscript tells the reader which kind of data you have, so it is never optional:

tTime series: Yt  where  t = 1990, 1991, …, 2012
iCross-sectional: Yi  where  i = 1, 2, 3, …, 40
itPanel: Yit  where  i and t are as defined above

4 · Handling data before you model it

Before getting into the statistical and econometric tools, a preliminary analysis is extremely important. The aim is to "get the feel" of your data, and there are four activities: looking at the raw data, graphical analysis, summary statistics, and data transformations.

Looking at the raw data

Compute the summary statistics — mean, mode, median, maximum, minimum. Then ask two questions that the summary statistics alone will not answer. Are there outliers, or are those extreme values measurement errors? And is there missingness — observations that are simply absent, and if so, are they absent for a reason that is related to what you are studying?

Graphical analysis

Graphs facilitate the inspection process and let you see the big picture. Three earn their place:

1Histograms indicate the distribution of a single variable, and make outliers obvious.
2Line graphs facilitate comparison of series, and are particularly useful for time series analysis.
3Scatter plots give combinations of values from two series, for determining their relationship — if any.

The scatter plot is the one to reach for first in this course. Every regression you estimate is, at bottom, a line drawn through a scatter plot, and looking at the scatter before estimating tells you whether a straight line was ever a sensible idea.

5 · Putting it together

Suppose you are studying the consumption function using aggregate household consumption in Barbados between 1970 and 2016. Three questions have to be answered before any software is opened: what is the model specification? what is the econometric specification? and what data will you use? Work through those and you have followed Stages 1 to 4 of this unit.

The full sequence of an applied econometric study looks like this:

  1. Decide what you want to doDescribe, test, forecast or evaluate — the four uses from section 1.
  2. Economic theory
  3. Econometric modelData feed in here.
  4. Model estimation
  5. Specification testing and diagnosticsAll of Unit 2 lives at this step.
  6. Is the model adequate?No → return to the econometric model and respecify.
  7. Test any hypothesisUnit 1F.
  8. Use for predictions and policy makingFindings feed back into economic theory, and the cycle begins again.

Two features of that diagram are easy to miss. It contains a loop: a model that fails its diagnostics sends you back to respecify, and this happens routinely rather than exceptionally. And it ends where it began — results feed back into economic theory, which is how a discipline learns.

In this course Econometrics I introduces you to the methods needed to conduct empirical analysis. The focus point is regression analysis, the starting point in econometrics. Unit 1B begins it in earnest.

6 · Tutorial

Draft questions, to be replaced by the tutorial sheet issued in class.

Explain, in your own words, why the mathematical model Y = β1 + β2X cannot be used to test Keynes' conjecture, while Yi = β1 + β2Xi + ui can.
Show solution

The mathematical model is deterministic: it asserts that every household with a given income consumes exactly β1 + β2X. Real data will contradict it at essentially every observation, so any test of it fails immediately, and fails for a reason that has nothing to do with whether Keynes was right.

Adding ui changes the claim being made. The econometric model says that the line is the average relationship, E(Y | X) = β1 + β2X, and that individual households depart from it for reasons the model does not capture. That is a claim data can be consistent with, and it makes β2 a quantity that can be estimated and tested against Keynes' prediction that 0 < β2 < 1.

List four distinct things the disturbance term may represent, and for each give an example in the context of a consumption function.
Show solution

Omitted variables — household wealth affects consumption but does not appear in the model. Incorrect functional form — the true consumption function may curve, so that fitting a straight line leaves a systematic remainder. Errors of measurement — survey respondents misreport their income, and the mismatch between reported and true income lands in u. Intrinsic randomness — two identical households simply make different choices in a given month.

The first two are the dangerous ones, because they are systematic rather than random. Both are taken up in Unit 2A.

For each of the following, state the type of dataset and write the dependent variable with the correct subscript: (a) the unemployment rate for Barbados for each quarter from 2000 to 2020; (b) the wages of 500 workers surveyed in Bridgetown in June; (c) GDP and inflation for 15 CARICOM members in each year from 2010 to 2020.
Show solution

(a) Time series — one unit, many periods: Yt, t = 2000Q1, …, 2020Q4.

(b) Cross-sectional — many units, one point in time: Yi, i = 1, 2, …, 500.

(c) Panel (longitudinal) — the same 15 countries followed over 11 years: Yit, i = 1, …, 15 and t = 2010, …, 2020. Note that if a different sample of countries were drawn each year, it would be pooled cross sections rather than a panel.

A researcher finds a strong positive relationship between ice cream sales and drowning deaths across months, using observational data. Explain what is wrong with concluding that ice cream causes drowning, and relate your answer to the contents of u.
Show solution

The data are observational, not experimental: nobody assigned ice cream consumption to months. With observational data, cause and effect are very hard to establish — associations can be investigated, but a coefficient is not an effect merely because it is significant.

In the language of this unit, temperature is an omitted variable. It drives both ice cream sales and swimming, so it sits inside u while also being related to the regressor. That is precisely the condition under which the disturbance stops being harmless — the case taken up formally in Unit 2A Part 1 as omitted-variable bias, and in Unit 3B as endogeneity.
